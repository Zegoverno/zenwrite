# Find notes

Help the writer discover useful material without changing the draft.

1. Identify the draft's main idea, claims needing support, and open questions.
2. Search the available note collection with three to six focused queries; do not read the whole collection.
3. Keep only results that materially help the draft. One narrower follow-up search is enough for a promising lead.
4. Propose at most six notes, most useful first; none is valid.

Each proposed note is one or two short sentences in the draft's language explaining how the linked note helps: evidence, example, source, counterpoint, or related idea. Use the collection's normal link syntax. Quote fewer than 30 words only when the wording itself matters. Keep religious, philosophical, and empirical support distinct when that distinction matters.
